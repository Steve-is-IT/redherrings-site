What makes an incident-response case different

A forensics case hands you an image and asks what happened. An IR case drops you into a live incident with the same tooling a responder actually has — and the same clock ticking.

01

Worked from the responder's own tooling

Students start where a real responder starts: an EDR detection feed (edr/detections.json and a matching CSV) and a SIEM alert export (siem/alerts.json and CSV), corroborated by host event logs. They triage the detections, pivot into the alerts, and confirm against the logs — no narrated story, just the evidence a SOC sees.

02

Mapped to ATT&CK, tagged by NIST phase

Every case declares the MITRE ATT&CK tactics and techniques it exercises, and every question is tagged with its NIST 800-61 phase — detection and analysis, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident. Pick a case by the behaviour you want learners to recognise, and grade them on whether they named the right technique and made the right call at the right phase.

03

Time pressure that teaches, plus a real timer

Each case carries a diegetic in-world response clock — the narrative urgency of an incident unfolding host by host — so learners feel why sequence and triage matter. On top of that, an optional instructor timer runs timed or exam mode with a server-enforced countdown. One is the story; the other is the stopwatch.

Answer types that make incident response gradable

IR has never graded cleanly as free text. Red Herrings adds answer types built for the work, so a machine can score triage judgement as fairly as a hash — alongside the usual text, hash, timestamp, IP and domain types.

01

MITRE ATT&CK, parent-aware

Name the technique behind an observed behaviour. Grading is ATT&CK-aware: a sub-technique answer is matched against its parent, so a reasonable-but-broader answer earns appropriate credit instead of a flat zero.

02

IOC, set-graded

Pull every indicator that matters from the feeds — the hashes, IPs and domains worth escalating — and submit them as a set. The grader scores the set for completeness and precision, rewarding the full picture rather than one lucky line.

03

Sequence, ordered with partial credit

Put the stages of the attack, or the steps of the response, in the right order. Sequence answers are graded for order with partial credit, so a learner who gets the shape right but swaps two steps is not treated the same as one who guessed.

04

Severity and boolean calls

Make the judgement the incident demands: rate the severity, or answer the yes/no containment question. These are the decisions a responder is paid to get right, and now they score automatically against the key.

Different for every student, verified before release

An incident is only a teachable one if everyone can't just share the answer. Red Herrings builds each IR lab from an independent seed and proves it before it ever reaches a student.

01

A different incident for every student

Hostnames, users, timestamps, IPs, hashes and the answers themselves all change per student from a single seed. No two people work the same detection feed, so a copied IOC or a borrowed ATT&CK technique is simply the wrong answer for that student.

02

Independently re-solved and verified

Every variant is independently re-solved and verified before release — the green Fresh Catch check. A fact that can't be found in that student's own feeds is flagged before you ever hand the lab out.

03

The answer key never reaches the browser

Grading is server-side and runs on your machine. The answer key is never sent to the student's browser, so there is nothing in the page to read, scrape or share.
