Built to be used by everyone

We want Red Herrings to work for every student and every instructor, including those who navigate by keyboard, use a screen reader, or need reduced motion. We build toward the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA, and this page sets out plainly what is in place today, where we fall short, and how to reach us.

Our commitment

We treat accessibility as part of the product, not an afterthought. We measure our work against WCAG 2.1 AA and improve it as we ship.

Red Herrings is a desktop application whose interface runs in your browser, plus a static marketing site. We aim for both to substantially conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. We have not yet completed a formal third-party audit, so we do not claim full, certified conformance. What follows is an honest account of the measures in place and the areas we are still working on. If something on this page gets out of date, the email addresses below reach a person who can help.

What is in place today

Concrete measures across the app and the site, the things an evaluator checks first.

01

Full keyboard operation

The app is operable without a mouse. A "Skip to main content" link is the first focusable element, interactive controls are reachable in a logical order, and keyboard shortcuts are documented in the in-app help.

02

Visible focus

A clear focus ring marks the focused control, driven by the browser's focus-visible behaviour, so keyboard users can always see where they are on the page.

03

Landmarks and current state

Navigation, main and complementary regions are marked with ARIA landmarks, the current section is announced with aria-current, and status changes use polite live regions so assistive technology is kept informed.

04

Reduced motion

When your system is set to reduce motion, the interface honours it: animations are collapsed, iteration counts are capped and smooth scrolling is turned off.

05

Readable structure and contrast

Pages use real headings in order, labelled form controls and descriptive link text. The colour system is built for AA contrast in both the light and dark themes, and the theme preference is remembered.

06

Works at zoom and on small screens

Layouts reflow rather than break: text stays readable when zoomed and the app and site adapt down to small and mobile viewports without a loss of content or function.

07

Grading does not depend on the browser

Answers are graded on your machine and only the result returns, so the solve experience does not rely on fragile client-side scripting or hidden visual cues to tell a student whether they were right.

08

Documents you can work with

Student worksheets are generated as tagged PDF and DOCX with real headings and text, so they can be read with assistive technology or enlarged, rather than delivered as flat images.

Where we fall short

We would rather tell you than let you find out. These are the areas we know need more work.

01

No formal audit yet

Our conformance is based on our own testing against WCAG 2.1 AA, not an independent third-party audit. A VPAT (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) is available on request and reflects that same basis.

02

Some evidence is inherently visual

A few artefacts in advanced cases, such as screenshots or packet-capture views, are visual by nature. Where a case depends on one we will work with you and your disability-services team on an equivalent; tell us the case and we will help.

03

Ongoing work

Accessibility is never finished. We retest as the interface changes and we prioritise fixes reported to us by real users of assistive technology.

Request a VPAT or tell us about a barrier

Need our VPAT or an accessibility questionnaire completed for a procurement or Section 508 review? Email sales@redherrings.app and tell us what your review needs. If you hit an accessibility barrier using Red Herrings, please report it to the same address with the page or case and what happened, and we will respond and work with you on a fix or an equivalent.
