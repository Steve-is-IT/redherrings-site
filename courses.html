Introduction to Digital Forensics

A 14-week undergraduate course: Windows artifacts, memory, network and mobile evidence, ending in a graded capstone.

14 weeksUndergraduate, second or third yearAbout 10 lab hours

Learning outcomes

  1. Acquire and verify evidence, and keep a chain of custody.
  2. Recover program execution, file, USB and persistence artifacts from a Windows system.
  3. Build and defend a timeline from several artifacts.
  4. Triage memory, network and mobile evidence with free tools.
  5. Write an investigation report that states findings, cites artifacts and explains method.

Assessment and tools

Weekly labs 50% (best 10 of 12), midterm practical 20%, capstone practical 20%, investigation report 10%.

Autopsy, Registry Explorer, EvtxECmd, PECmd, LECmd, JLECmd, Volatility 3, Wireshark, DB Browser for SQLite, Timesketch. All free.

Prerequisites: Operating systems fundamentals and basic networking. No prior forensics.

Incident Response Fundamentals

An 8-week course from reading one EDR alert to running a ransomware incident through every NIST 800-61 phase.

8 weeksUpper undergraduate, graduate, or professional cohortAbout 6 lab hours

Learning outcomes

  1. Triage EDR, SIEM and proxy evidence by severity and ATT&CK technique.
  2. Scope an intrusion from initial access to impact.
  3. Choose and justify containment, eradication and recovery actions.
  4. Produce indicators and a breach report a manager can act on.

Assessment and tools

Weekly labs 50% (best 6 of 7), timed drills 30%, final incident 20%.

Any JSON viewer, jq, Excel or LibreOffice, Wireshark, EvtxECmd, Timeline Explorer. All free.

Prerequisites: Networking and operating systems fundamentals. Introduction to Digital Forensics is helpful but not required.
