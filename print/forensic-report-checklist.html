What a defensible forensic report contains

A section-by-section checklist for the written part of an investigation, usable as a template for students and as a grading guide for instructors. The order is the order a reader needs it: conclusion first, proof after, detail last.

1. Front matter

  • Case identifier, report date, report version
  • Examiner name, role and contact; organization
  • Requesting party and the question they asked, in their words
  • Classification or handling marking if any

2. Executive summary (half a page)

  • What was examined, in one sentence
  • Each conclusion as a plain statement with a confidence level
  • What could not be determined and why
  • No tool names, no hashes, no jargon the requester would not use

3. Scope and authority

  • Items received, each with number, description and hash
  • Who collected them, when, and the chain of custody reference
  • Questions in scope; questions explicitly out of scope
  • Authority to examine (consent, warrant, policy, course assignment)

4. Methodology

  • Tools with versions; verification that the image hash matched before analysis
  • Write-blocking or working-copy procedure
  • Time zone used throughout, and how it was established
  • Any deviation from standard procedure, with the reason

5. Findings

  • One finding per heading, each answering one question from the scope
  • Every factual claim tied to a specific artifact: path, record, offset or event, and a timestamp
  • Screenshots or extracts only where they prove something; captioned
  • Observation and interpretation kept in separate sentences
  • Alternative explanations considered and, where possible, ruled out

6. Timeline

  • Chronological table: time (one zone), source artifact, event, significance
  • Only events that support a finding; the full export goes in an appendix
  • Gaps and clock problems called out, not smoothed over

7. Conclusions

  • Restate each question and answer it directly
  • Confidence stated and justified by the findings section
  • Limitations: missing evidence, anti-forensics, time
  • Recommendations only if asked for them

8. Appendices

  • Evidence manifest with hashes; chain of custody forms
  • Full tool output referenced in the findings
  • Glossary for the non-technical reader
  • Examiner qualifications
Common ways student reports lose marks. Conclusions that are not in the findings. A finding with no artifact behind it. Mixed time zones. "The malware" when the evidence shows "an executable named". A summary written last and longest instead of first and shortest. Screenshots of a whole screen when one line was the point.
